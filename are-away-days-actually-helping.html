Are away days actually helping people do anything differently?

Practical guidance on designing away days that create real, lasting change in behaviour and practice.

🏷 Richard Budd 📅 Aug 21, 2026 ⏱ 4 min read

Leadership Employee Engagement Learning
← Back to Resources

Away days and company updates can build connection and clarity, but the real test is what happens afterwards. Are people clear about how they contribute, and does the strategy show up in team discussions, objectives and everyday decisions?

Why many away days don’t lead to change

Many away days generate energy but lack mechanisms to transfer that energy into everyday practice. Common issues include unclear objectives, no named owners for follow-up, and a focus on inspiration rather than practical change.

Design choices that improve impact

  • Be specific about the behaviour or decision the away day should influence.
  • Include the people who will need to support and sustain changes (line managers, team leads).
  • Turn ideas into small, time-bound experiments with measurable success criteria.
  • Schedule short follow-ups (2 weeks, 6 weeks) and protect time in diaries for them.

Practical next steps

Start small: pick one behaviour to test, agree success measures, assign an owner, and check back quickly. Small wins help sustain momentum and demonstrate value to the wider organisation.
