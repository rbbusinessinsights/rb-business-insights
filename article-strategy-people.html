Why Business Strategy Means Nothing if Your People Don't Understand It

How to bridge the gap between leadership intent and employee understanding

✍️ By Richard Budd 📅 August 6, 2026 ⏱ 8 min read

Leadership Strategy Communication
← Back to Resources

Many organisations can tell you exactly what their sales targets are.

They know their profit margins, growth ambitions, recruitment forecasts, and customer acquisition goals. Leadership teams spend hours analysing performance data, discussing market opportunities, and setting ambitious plans for the future.

Yet ask people elsewhere in the business a simple question:

"Where is the organisation heading, and what part do you play in helping it get there?"

Far too often, the answer is less clear than it should be.

The reality is that strategy is only valuable when people understand it. A business plan sitting in a boardroom, a slide deck launched at an annual conference, or a set of objectives discussed only by senior leaders rarely changes organisational performance on its own.

People do.

And that's why helping people understand the direction of the organisation should be viewed as a business-critical activity rather than a communication exercise.

The Missing Link Between Strategy and Success

Throughout my work with organisations of all sizes, one theme consistently emerges when speaking to employees.

People want to understand the bigger picture.

They want to know where the organisation is going, why certain decisions are being made, and how their individual role contributes to achieving success.

When that connection exists, something powerful happens.

People stop simply completing tasks and start making contributions.

  • Decisions improve because people understand the intent behind them
  • Teams collaborate more effectively because they recognise how their work affects others
  • Innovation increases because people feel connected to outcomes rather than processes

Most importantly, people become invested in the organisation's success because they can see themselves within it.

Unfortunately, many businesses underestimate the importance of creating this connection.

The Obsession With Growth

There is an irony that exists in many organisations.

Leadership teams spend significant time discussing how to attract new customers, win new contracts, increase revenue, and grow market share.

All of these things matter.

However, considerably less energy is often invested in the people who actually deliver those results.

Businesses can become so focused on winning the next opportunity that they forget to invest in the people responsible for creating the current one.

It's not uncommon to see organisations spending thousands on sales campaigns while employees remain unclear about organisational priorities.

Or investing heavily in recruitment while overlooking the retention of experienced, high-performing individuals already within the business.

Growth matters. But sustainable growth comes from people.

  • Customers stay because of people
  • Service improves because of people
  • Innovation happens because of people
  • Reputation is built because of people

Every number on a performance dashboard is ultimately influenced by human behaviour somewhere in the organisation.

People Support What They Help Create

One of the greatest mistakes leaders make is assuming that communication equals involvement.

Sharing a strategy is not the same as involving people in shaping it.

The most successful organisations create environments where people feel connected to organisational goals and have opportunities to influence how those goals are achieved.

This doesn't mean every decision should be made by committee.

It means creating meaningful opportunities for people to:

  • Contribute ideas
  • Challenge thinking
  • Identify risks
  • Offer solutions

When people help shape the future, they feel ownership of it. They become advocates rather than observers.

That's when engagement moves beyond surveys and becomes embedded in everyday working life.

Making Strategy Relevant

One of the reasons strategy often fails to connect is because it is communicated at too high a level.

Vision statements and corporate objectives may make sense to leaders, but people need help understanding what they mean in practical terms.

Three Questions Everyone Should Answer

Every employee should be able to confidently answer these three questions:

  • What is the organisation trying to achieve?
  • How does my role contribute to that success?
  • What do I need to focus on over the next 90 days to make a positive impact?

If people cannot answer those questions confidently, there is likely a disconnect somewhere between leadership intention and employee understanding.

The best organisations work relentlessly to close that gap.

  • They communicate frequently
  • They encourage questions
  • They revisit priorities regularly
  • They help individuals connect daily activities to wider organisational outcomes

The Retention Advantage

There is another important reason why this matters.

People are more likely to stay where they feel connected.

Employees who understand the purpose of the organisation, believe their contribution matters, and feel their voice is valued generally show greater commitment than those who simply turn up to perform a task.

Retention is rarely driven by salary alone.

People stay because they feel part of something worthwhile. They stay because they see opportunities to contribute. They stay because leaders genuinely listen.

In a world where recruitment costs continue to rise and skills shortages remain a challenge, retaining great people is often a far more effective business strategy than constantly replacing them.

The Challenge for Leaders

If you're a leader, ask yourself a simple question.

How much time have you invested this month discussing targets and results?

Now compare that to the time you've invested helping people understand the direction of the organisation, listening to their ideas, and demonstrating how they contribute to success.

The answer may be revealing.

Because while growth targets are important, they do not deliver themselves.

The organisations that consistently outperform are rarely those with the most impressive strategy documents.

They are the ones where people understand the direction, believe in it, and are empowered to help shape the journey.

Your people are not separate from your strategy.

They are your strategy.
